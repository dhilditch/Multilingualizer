## How the Weglot price calculation works

The basic calculation is:

**Source words × destination languages = translated words**

If your site contains 12,000 source words and you want French and German, your working estimate is 24,000 translated words.

Weglot's published allowance is based on translated words and the number of translated languages. A plan has to cover both. A 9,000-word site needs 18,000 translated words for two destination languages, so a 10,000-word plan would not be sufficient even though the original site contains fewer than 10,000 words.

The calculator is deliberately an estimate. Weglot counts unique translated words, while a basic site crawl can count repeated navigation and footer text more than once. The estimate can therefore run high. It can also run low if important pages are behind a login, generated by JavaScript, missing from the site's internal links, or not included in the ten-page audit.

Weglot also counts translatable SEO content such as page titles, meta descriptions and image alt text. Images, videos, PDF files and URLs do not add to the word count simply by existing, although text associated with them may count. See Weglot's current explanation of [how translated words are counted](https://support.weglot.com/article/59-total-number-translated-words).

## 12 things to know before using Weglot with Squarespace

### 1. The built-in integration is for Squarespace 7.1

Squarespace's current documentation says its built-in Weglot integration is available for version 7.1 sites. If your site is on Squarespace 7.0, do not buy a plan on the assumption that the same setup instructions apply.

Check your Squarespace version first. If you are on 7.0, confirm the current supported route with Weglot or decide whether a move to 7.1 is sensible for the site as a whole. Translation should not be the excuse for an unplanned platform migration.

### 2. Not every Squarespace area is translated

The main website can be translated without every connected Squarespace service following it.

Squarespace currently lists these as not translated by the Weglot integration:

- Squarespace Email Campaigns
- Acuity Scheduling
- third-party blocks such as map blocks
- Member Sites
- customer login screens

That distinction matters. A brochure site may be covered well. A membership site or appointment business may still send visitors into an English-only part of the journey.

Write down the complete customer journey before you sign up: landing page, form, account, appointment, checkout, confirmation and follow-up email. Mark which system owns each step. Test every important step in every language you intend to sell in.

### 3. Your estimate needs more than visible paragraph text

Counting the words in ten page bodies is a useful start, but it is not the final billable total.

Include:

- navigation and footer text
- product and service descriptions
- blog posts and category pages
- page titles and meta descriptions
- image alt text
- form labels and validation messages that the integration can see

Weglot says translations are generated progressively when a page is visited by a user or bot. Translated content remains in the translations list unless you remove it manually. In practice, old and test content can affect the total even when it is no longer prominent on the live site.

### 4. Every destination language multiplies the working word count

This is the easiest pricing detail to underestimate.

Adding Spanish to a 20,000-word English site creates a working estimate of 20,000 translated words. Adding Spanish, French and German creates 60,000. That moves the same source site through different plan levels without a single new English page being published.

Start with languages that have a commercial reason to exist. Traffic, enquiries, current buyers and sales territories are better evidence than a large language switcher.

### 5. Language subdomains are part of the SEO setup

Weglot can use language subdomains such as `fr.example.com` and `de.example.com`. Squarespace recommends this setup because it gives each language an indexable home, supports language annotations and avoids relying entirely on in-page JavaScript switching.

Weglot provides the DNS records. Where you add them depends on who manages the domain:

- for a Squarespace-managed domain, add the supplied CNAME records in Squarespace DNS settings
- for a third-party domain, add them with that domain or DNS provider

Squarespace does not invent or automatically configure every external DNS record for you. Follow the current [Weglot Squarespace subdomain instructions](https://support.weglot.com/article/243-squarespace-integration-setup), then verify each language hostname before launch.

You need a custom domain to use language subdomains. Include DNS access in the launch checklist rather than discovering that requirement on the day you want to publish.

### 6. Machine translation still needs review

Weglot gives you a fast first translation and an interface for editing it. It does not remove the need to review pages that make claims, explain prices or ask for payment.

Prioritise the text that can cause an expensive misunderstanding:

1. product names and positioning
2. pricing and units
3. guarantees, delivery and returns
4. calls to action and forms
5. checkout and confirmation text
6. title tags and search snippets

You edit translations in Weglot, not in the normal Squarespace page editor. Decide who owns those edits and how changes to the original page will be checked later.

### 7. Your plan needs headroom

Do not choose a plan that only works while the site stays exactly as it is today.

New blog posts, products, landing pages and destination languages increase usage. Leave room for the next few months of planned content. If the estimate is close to a limit, crawl the site more carefully and check Weglot's own dashboard before committing to annual billing.

Weglot and Squarespace document different behaviour when an account exceeds its allowance depending on whether it is free or paid and how the account is configured. The safe assumption is that exceeding a limit can interrupt new translations or what visitors can see. Check the current overage and automatic-upgrade settings in the Weglot account rather than relying on an old article.

### 8. Test dynamic and third-party content separately

A crawler can inspect the HTML it receives. It cannot promise that every script, popup, embedded widget or application state will be translated correctly.

Make a short test matrix:

| Area | What to test |
|---|---|
| Navigation | desktop, mobile and dropdown labels |
| Forms | labels, placeholders, validation and success messages |
| Commerce | products, basket, checkout, tax and confirmation |
| Popups | trigger, text, close controls and mobile layout |
| Embeds | booking, maps, reviews, chat and video controls |
| Accounts | login, password reset and member-only pages |

The unsupported items in Squarespace's documentation should be treated as known boundaries. Everything else still needs testing on your particular site.

### 9. Translation does not localise your whole offer

A French page can still show prices only in pounds, quote delivery times for the UK and send buyers to an English-only support flow.

Translation is one part of localisation. Depending on the business, also check:

- currency display and the currency used at checkout
- tax and delivery information
- measurements and date formats
- contact hours and telephone numbers
- market-specific products or legal text

If the site sells internationally, our Multicurrencyalizer work may be relevant to localised price display. That is a separate job from translation and needs its own compatibility and checkout testing.

### 10. Search optimisation needs translated intent, not just translated words

A literal translation of the English target phrase is not always what people search for in another market.

For important landing pages, research the query in the destination language. Then review the translated title, heading, description, URL and internal links together. The person reviewing it needs to understand both the language and the product.

The technical pieces still matter: indexable language URLs, correct canonicals, `hreflang`, internal links and an XML sitemap that exposes the pages you expect search engines to find. Verify the rendered result instead of assuming the switcher has handled every SEO detail.

### 11. Disconnecting the integration does not cancel the subscription

Squarespace's guide explicitly separates disconnecting Weglot from cancelling Weglot billing. If you stop using the integration, deal with both parts.

Before launch, also consider the exit path:

- where the edited translations are stored
- what happens to translated URLs if you cancel
- whether valuable translated pages need redirects
- how you would move reviewed translations to another system

This is normal supplier planning. It is much easier to answer before the translated pages have earned links and rankings.

### 12. Weglot is not automatically the right answer for every site

Weglot is a sensible fit when the site owner values a managed translation workflow, wants to launch quickly and accepts an ongoing plan linked to translated words and languages.

A manual approach can make more sense for a very small site, a site with one carefully maintained translation, or an owner who wants direct control over every translated page and cost. Squarespace's own page duplication approach and tools such as Multilingualizer come with different maintenance and compatibility trade-offs.

The useful question is not “Which tool has the longest feature list?” It is “Which workflow can this business maintain after launch?”

## A practical decision sequence

Use this order:

1. Confirm the site is Squarespace 7.1.
2. List every part of the customer journey, including third-party services.
3. Estimate source words from the full public site.
4. Multiply by the destination languages you can justify commercially.
5. Check the plan's word and language limits.
6. Confirm DNS access and the language-subdomain setup.
7. Test the unsupported, dynamic and commercial parts of the journey.
8. Assign a human reviewer for high-value translated pages.
9. Measure enquiries, affiliate clicks or sales by language after launch.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Squarespace create the Weglot language subdomains for me?

Weglot gives you the required DNS entries. If Squarespace manages your domain, you add the CNAME records in Squarespace's DNS settings. If another provider manages DNS, you add them there. You then return to Weglot to check the configuration.

### Does Weglot charge once for a word or once per language?

For planning, multiply your source-word estimate by the number of destination languages. Weglot describes the allowance as total unique translated words across those languages. Repeated page views do not repeatedly consume the allowance.

### Can the calculator find every word on my Squarespace site?

No. The quick check reads one public homepage. The emailed audit checks up to ten same-host public HTML pages that it can discover. It will not reliably find gated pages, unlinked pages, all JavaScript-rendered text or content owned by a third-party service. Treat it as a planning estimate and compare it with Weglot's account count.

### Does Weglot translate Squarespace checkout and account pages?

Do not assume the whole journey is covered. Squarespace lists Member Sites and customer login screens among the areas not translated by the integration, and third-party services can have their own limitations. Test the exact commerce or account journey you use.

### Is Weglot's trial 10 days or 14 days?

At the time of checking, Squarespace's help page and Weglot's own pricing page did not state the same duration. Treat Weglot's live signup and pricing terms as the current offer rather than relying on either number in an older guide.
