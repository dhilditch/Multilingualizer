Webflow's published Localize prices start at $9 per month based on added locales for Essential and $29 for Advanced. The $29 tier isn't the only option. Before comparing alternatives, check which tier your site needs and the billing terms shown in your account.

A bilingual site normally needs one added locale beside its primary language. A site with English, French and German needs two. Don't count the primary language as another translation purchase.

## Work out the localisation budget

The following uses the displayed per-locale rates on [Webflow's pricing page](https://webflow.com/pricing), checked on 3 October 2026. It shows the localisation component only, not a complete website bill.

| Added locales | Essential monthly equivalent | Advanced monthly equivalent |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | $9 | $29 |
| 2 | $18 | $58 |
| 3 | $27 | $87 |

At the same rate for twelve months, one added locale would total $108 for Essential or $348 for Advanced. Those figures are budget arithmetic, not a promise of monthly billing. Confirm the commitment, taxes, existing Site plan and any other charges at checkout.

List your required features before deciding that a plan is too expensive. Paying for a tier you don't need and choosing a cheaper tool that doesn't meet the brief are both avoidable mistakes.

## What are you buying with native Localize?

Webflow's native route covers static and CMS content, locale subdirectories and localised search metadata. Essential and Advanced have different capabilities. Use the current feature comparison rather than assuming every feature is included at $9.

There is also an important shop limitation. [Webflow's Localize overview](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961240752147-Webflow-Localize-overview) says Localize doesn't support Ecommerce Site plan features. Check products, categories, pages and checkout separately if you're selling online.

## Compare alternatives by the work they save

### Separately maintained language pages

If you're considering a manual approach, first work out how you'll organise each language, navigation and updates. Build one test page in each language. Check that visitors can switch between matching pages and that your editor can maintain the structure without overwriting either version.

The hosting bill isn't the whole cost. Record the time needed to review changes across every language. A small brochure site and a frequently updated catalogue need different maintenance budgets.

### A third-party translation service

For a managed service, compare the word allowance, language count, review workflow and supported content. Ask how language URLs, forms and externally hosted screens are handled on your exact site.

The [Weglot pricing calculator](/weglot-pricing-calculator-squarespace/) can estimate a word-and-language budget. Its implementation guide is Squarespace-specific, so don't use those setup instructions as Webflow instructions. Confirm the vendor's Webflow integration separately.

## Check one representative page before committing

Use a page with navigation, a form and a CMS item if your site uses those features. Include a product journey if it sells anything.

1. List the languages and regions you actually need.
2. Check the current native tier and its complete bill.
3. Decide whether separate language URLs are required.
4. Test the proposed alternative against the same page and journey.
5. Assign responsibility for translation review and later updates.

For search-led language versions, [Google recommends separate URLs](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites). A visible language switcher alone doesn't establish that search engines can index each version.
