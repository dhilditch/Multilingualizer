There is plenty of advice about making Squarespace multilingual. There is much less evidence about who actually does it.

I have sold Multilingualizer to 1,202 customers since 2014. In August 2026 I checked the public websites supplied with those purchases to see which sites still existed, which platform they used and which multilingual setup was visible.

This article reports the aggregate findings. It does not publish customer emails, order values or private account data.

## The short version

- 821 customer sites were reachable and still represented the customer's business
- those sites belonged to 674 unique customers
- 418 of the reachable sites used Squarespace, belonging to 337 customers
- 490 reachable sites still exposed a multilingual implementation
- Multilingualizer itself was still detectable on 297 reachable sites
- 267 of those Multilingualizer installations were on Squarespace
- historic customers came from 79 recorded billing countries
- English/French was the most common recorded language combination, followed by English/German and English/Spanish

At customer level, Multilingualizer was still detectable for 214 of the 337 customers with a reachable Squarespace site. That is 63.5%.

That figure needs context. A public crawl can prove that a script is present. It cannot prove that every language has been maintained, that the owner is satisfied or that every part of the visitor journey is translated.

## How the study was built

The source population is 1,202 unique customers with at least one paid order. Some customers supplied more than one website, giving 1,381 installation records.

Each supplied URL was checked using a rate-limited public crawl. The crawler inspected the homepage and, where available, an about or contact page. It recorded:

- whether the site was reachable
- whether the domain still represented the customer's business
- the detected website platform
- visible multilingual controls and scripts
- visible third-party technology
- public page text, services, currencies and prices
- a deterministic sector and business-model classification

A parked domain, an unrelated redirected business or an unavailable site was not treated as a current customer site.

The important denominators are therefore different:

| Question | Denominator |
|---|---:|
| Where did historic customers buy from? | 1,202 customers |
| What do currently reachable customer sites use? | 674 customers / 821 sites |
| What do current Squarespace sites use? | 337 customers / 418 sites |

This distinction matters. Saying that 17 customers use Elfsight out of all 1,202 historic buyers would mix live, dead and moved sites. The current-technology denominator is 674 customers whose sites could actually be checked.

Billing country is not the same thing as audience location. A Swiss business may sell internationally, and a Canadian buyer may be building a site for somebody else. These figures describe the customer record, not visitor traffic.

These sectors share a practical reason for translating. Their product is often explained rather than merely added to a basket: a course, architectural service, hotel, event, treatment, menu or collection.

The business-model classification shows the same pattern. Excluding unclassified sites, professional services was the largest group with 88 Squarespace installations, followed by ecommerce with 65, agencies and consultancies with 29, and portfolios or studios with 25.

## How many still use Multilingualizer?

Across all 821 reachable customer sites:

- 490 sites still exposed some form of multilingual implementation
- 297 sites still exposed Multilingualizer specifically
- those Multilingualizer sites belonged to 239 unique customers

Within the Squarespace subset:

- 267 of 418 reachable sites still exposed Multilingualizer
- those installations belonged to 214 of 337 customers

The site-level Squarespace rate is 63.9%. The customer-level rate is 63.5%.

This does not mean the other customers abandoned multilingual content. Some now use Weglot, WPML, TranslatePress, platform-native tools or a custom setup. Others have a reachable site where the crawler did not find current multilingual evidence.

## What the study cannot tell us

The crawl has real limits:

- it checks public pages, not protected member, account or checkout screens
- a consent banner or ad blocker can prevent a technology fingerprint from loading
- some tools run only on inner pages that were not crawled
- recorded registration languages may have changed since purchase
- sector classification uses public text and technology signals, and 115 Squarespace sites remain unclassified
- a detectable installation does not prove satisfaction, revenue or conversion performance

I will not publish claims such as “this increased sales” unless the business supplies the underlying numbers and approves the wording.
