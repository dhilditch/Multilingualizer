1. Decide who will maintain the translations

If you're bilingual and have a small brochure, portfolio or service site, maintaining your translated text yourself can be straightforward. With Multilingualizer, you enter each language in the page editor using the translation markers. You don't need to learn Weglot's interface.

Count the work as well as the pages. A ten-page website that changes once a year is a different job from a shop with frequent product launches. Write down who will translate new content, review it and keep each language current.

2. Decide whether each language needs its own search landing pages

Switching the visible language on one URL isn't the same as publishing a separate URL for each language. Google recommends different URLs for language versions and warns that it might not crawl every version when content changes according to browser settings or cookies. Neither approach guarantees rankings.

For Weglot on Squarespace, follow the language subdomain guide. Don't assume a JavaScript-only installation gives you the same result. For the search checks, use the Squarespace multilingual SEO guide.

3. Test the entire enquiry or purchase journey

A translated product description doesn't prove the checkout, account screen or confirmation email is translated. Squarespace lists exclusions from its Weglot integration, including Email Campaigns, Acuity Scheduling, third-party content blocks, Member Sites and customer account login screens.

Use the checkout, forms and emails guide to plan your tests. Check the actual route your visitors take: product or service page, enquiry form or basket, checkout where relevant, then confirmation. Record which language appears at each step. Don't treat “I have a shop” as an automatic recommendation for either product.
