To make a website bilingual, choose how you'll maintain the two languages, translate the pages your visitors need and give them a clear way to switch. Before buying a tool, decide whether each language needs its own searchable URL or whether switching the text on the same page suits your site.

Start with your homepage and one important service or product page. Test the complete journey before translating the rest. A translated introduction isn't much help if the enquiry form or buying instructions are still unreadable to that visitor.

## 1. Check your website builder

Find the exact platform and plan you're using. A domain registrar isn't necessarily your website builder, and a custom HTML block isn't proof that a script can control every part of the site.

## 2. Choose your translation workflow

There are three common routes:

- **Separate language pages.** You maintain each language version and link between them. This gives each version its own page, but changes need reviewing across both versions.
- **Your own translations on the same page.** You write the text and use a language switcher to control what visitors see. This suits owners who want editorial control inside their existing editor.
- **A managed translation service.** The service provides an initial translation and a place to review it. Check its costs, language URLs and coverage of forms, accounts and checkout.

For multilingual search traffic, [Google recommends separate URLs for language versions](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites). Don't assume that switching visible text creates separately indexable language pages. Use the [Squarespace multilingual SEO guide](/squarespace-weglot-seo/) if separate language URLs are part of your plan.

## 3. Prepare the content that needs translating

List the pages visitors use to understand your offer, contact you or buy. Include navigation, buttons, form labels, product details and important emails, not only paragraphs.

Assign someone to review the second language. Keep a list of approved product names and terms. When the original text changes, record which translated pages need updating.

If you're considering Weglot, use the [pricing calculator](/weglot-pricing-calculator-squarespace/) to estimate words and languages before choosing a subscription. Check the live plan and the integration's coverage before paying.

## 4. Add your translations and a language switcher

Follow the instructions for your chosen tool and platform. Keep the language order consistent and test the published page, not only the editor preview.

For Multilingualizer on Squarespace, the [installation guide](/support/how-to-install-the-multilingualizer-on-squarespace/) explains account configuration and code placement. The [on-page translation instructions](/support/how-to-add-on-page-translations/) explain the punctuation markers. It doesn't use `[en]...[/en]` wrappers for ordinary translated text.

For example, a short English/French heading can be written as `Welcome..Bienvenue`. Longer text blocks use four full stops at their beginning and end, with two full stops separating language versions. Follow the linked instructions for the complete format.

## 5. Test the visitor journey

Open the published site on desktop and mobile. Choose each language and check:

- navigation and language-switcher labels;
- page headings, images and buttons;
- form labels, validation and confirmation messages;
- product details, basket and checkout where applicable;
- account, booking and other externally provided screens;
- links to the correct language pages if you maintain separate versions.

Use the [multilingual launch checklist](/squarespace-multilingual-launch-checklist/) for a more detailed Squarespace review. Test external services separately rather than assuming the page translation reaches them.

## What should you do first?

Choose the workflow, translate one useful page and test it with someone who reads the second language. Once that works, repeat it across the pages that matter to your visitors. Keep translation review in your normal content-update process.
