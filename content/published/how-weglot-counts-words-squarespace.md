Weglot does not price a Squarespace site by page count or traffic. Its plan limits are based mainly on translated words and destination languages.

That sounds simple until a ten-page site contains shared navigation, product data, old translations and metadata that were not in your manual count.

The safest approach is to build an estimate, understand what the estimate misses, then compare it with the Weglot dashboard during the trial.

## The basic calculation

Start with:

**source words × destination languages = estimated translated words**

If the original English site contains 8,000 words:

- French adds about 8,000 translated words
- French and German add about 16,000
- French, German and Spanish add about 24,000

The original language is not another destination language. Traffic also does not repeatedly consume words.

## What Weglot includes

Weglot says its count includes visible and invisible translatable content, including:

- headings and paragraphs
- navigation and footer text
- buttons and labels
- product names and descriptions
- blog content
- page titles
- meta descriptions
- image alt attributes
- other text detected and translated on the site

Media files and URLs do not add words simply because they exist. Text associated with them can count.

## Repeated text and unique translations

Weglot associates a translation with one or more URLs. If the same text and HTML structure have already been translated, it can reuse the existing translation rather than creating a new one for every page view.

That is one reason a crawler's raw page total may be higher than the Weglot dashboard. A crawler can count the same navigation on ten pages ten times, while Weglot may reuse that translated segment.

Do not compensate by simply deleting every repeated line from your estimate. Treat the crawl as a planning range and the dashboard as the billable observation.

## Translations are generated progressively

Weglot does not necessarily translate every URL the moment you connect it. Translations are generated when a visitor, search engine or site owner visits the page in the destination language, or when you synchronise URLs through Weglot's URL management.

An early dashboard count can therefore be too low. Visit the full site in each destination language or synchronise the intended URLs before using the count to choose an annual plan.

## Old translations can remain in the total

Weglot retains translations in the dashboard until you remove them. Old landing pages, demo content, test products and deleted copy can continue to matter if their translations remain.

Squarespace recommends finishing the site, removing demo content and considering exclusions before translating. That is sensible. Do not pay to translate a template page you are about to delete.

## Customer notification emails can add words

Squarespace enables translated customer notification emails by default when Weglot is connected. Those emails are useful for a store but unnecessary for a brochure site.

If you do not sell online, disable multilingual customer notifications before using the final count. If you do sell online, keep them and include their value in the plan rather than disabling them solely to reduce a number.

## Why a site crawl and Weglot can disagree

### The crawl is higher

Likely causes include:

- repeated menus and footers counted per page
- duplicate archive views
- crawler-visible text that Weglot reuses as one translation
- pages included in the crawl but excluded in Weglot

### The Weglot dashboard is higher

Likely causes include:

- translated metadata not included in the crawl
- dynamic content
- private pages reached through the site or dashboard
- old translations still stored
- customer notification emails
- more destination languages than the estimate used
- several projects sharing one account allowance

## A better estimating process

1. List every public page, product, blog post and important archive.
2. Remove demo, test and obsolete content.
3. Count visible source words across the site.
4. Add a margin for metadata and dynamic content that the crawl cannot see.
5. Multiply by the number of destination languages.
6. Note any pages or blocks you genuinely intend to exclude.
7. Start the trial and generate translations for all intended URLs.
8. Compare the dashboard total with the estimate.
9. Investigate the difference rather than rounding it away.
10. Leave space for planned products and articles.

## Do not optimise the wrong thing

Removing a useful product explanation to stay below a plan limit is usually false economy. Exclude pages that do not need translation, remove obsolete content and reuse variables where appropriate. Keep the copy that helps somebody buy or use the product correctly.

If the site sits just under a limit before launch, budget for growth. A new destination language multiplies the source content again.
