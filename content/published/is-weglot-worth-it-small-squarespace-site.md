Weglot can be worth it for a small Squarespace site, but page count is not the deciding factor.

A five-page site that needs indexable French pages, translated customer emails and regular copy changes may get more value from Weglot than a twenty-page brochure site whose owner already has the translations and changes them twice a year.

The useful comparison is not "monthly fee versus free". It is the ongoing fee versus the translation, SEO and maintenance work the service handles.

## Weglot is probably worth it when

- you want machine translation as a first draft
- you want separate translated URLs for multilingual SEO
- you want a dashboard for editing and reviewing translations
- the site changes often
- several people need to work on translations
- you run Squarespace Commerce and need translated customer notifications
- you accept an ongoing plan based on translated words and languages
- the documented unsupported Squarespace features are not central to the site

The strongest case is not that Weglot saves the initial copy-and-paste work. It is that it gives the site a repeatable translation workflow as pages change.

## Weglot may not be worth it when

- the site has very little text and one manually maintained translation
- you do not need translated pages indexed separately
- you already have approved translations
- the copy rarely changes
- the important journey runs through Acuity, Member Sites or another unsupported service
- the monthly fee matters more than automation
- you are willing to maintain the language logic yourself

A cheap plan is still poor value if the one feature you need is outside the integration.

## Check whether the free plan genuinely fits

Weglot's own help documentation says the free plan can cover one destination language and up to 2,000 translated words after the trial.

That is a small allowance. Include navigation, footer text, metadata and any customer notification email content that is translated. Generate the intended pages during the trial before assuming the site fits under 2,000.

If the limit is exceeded on the free plan, Weglot says translations stop displaying until the count is reduced or the plan is upgraded.

## What you are paying for

### Translation workflow

Weglot detects content, creates translations and stores them in its dashboard for review. That is useful when the original site changes regularly.

A manual method makes the owner responsible for noticing every changed paragraph, button and metadata field.

### Multilingual SEO infrastructure

With a server-side subdomain or subdirectory setup, each language has a separate URL. Weglot adds language relationships and serves translated source to crawlers.

Google recommends separate language URLs and `hreflang`. If target-language search traffic is part of the business case, this is a material benefit.

### Customer notification emails

Squarespace says translated customer notifications are enabled by default when Weglot is connected. For a store, that can remove a separate operational job.

Do not confuse this with Squarespace Email Campaigns, which the integration does not translate.

### Ongoing maintenance

The plan is not only paying for the words translated on launch day. It is paying for the system that notices, stores and serves later changes.

If the site never changes, this benefit has less value. If products, blog posts and offers change every week, it has more.

## What Weglot still does not do for you

It does not remove the need to:

- review machine translations
- approve product, policy and payment wording
- test every form and checkout state
- configure DNS for translated URLs
- monitor word limits
- manage unsupported Squarespace services separately
- check analytics and search performance

Weglot makes the work manageable. It does not make the site owner's responsibility disappear.

## Three small-site examples

### Five-page consultant site, English and French

The owner has approved French copy, changes the site twice a year and does not need French search traffic. A manual one-time tool or duplicated Squarespace pages may be enough.

### Eight-page local business site targeting another-language searches

Separate crawlable language URLs, translated metadata and `hreflang` matter. Weglot is easier to justify even if the visible word count is small.

### Ten-product store

Products, variants, checkout states and customer emails increase the scope. Weglot is likely the better starting point, provided the exact store journey passes testing.

## A five-question decision

1. Do you need separate translated URLs indexed in Google?
2. Will the original content change regularly?
3. Do you need machine translation and a review workflow?
4. Does the important visitor journey avoid Weglot's documented Squarespace exclusions?
5. Is the time saved worth the recurring plan at your real translated-word count?

If most answers are yes, Weglot is probably worth it. If most are no, compare the manual options before adding another monthly bill.
